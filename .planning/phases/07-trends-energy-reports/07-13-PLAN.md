## Phase Goal

**As an** engineer, **I want** a cost figure that is either right or refused, **so that** I am never handed one that is wrong by a factor of a thousand.

<objective>Validate units and state exclusions before arithmetic.</objective>

<tasks>
<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: Units are checked, not displayed</name>
  <files>custom_components/glt_flow_card/energy_units.py</files>
  <action>Fix D15 and T7-11. Today the unit is read from the entity for display and never compared against anything, so a meter in `Wh` and one in `kWh` contribute to the same euro total three orders of magnitude apart. Validate the entity's unit against the meter's declared unit, against the model it is bound to, and against the unit the price is denominated in. A convertible pair converts through a declared factor; an incompatible pair is **refused with a reason**. Phase 5 established that refusing beats degrading, and a wrong cost figure is a worse degradation than a missing one.</action>
  <acceptance_criteria>
    - An incompatible unit pair is refused with a reason from a closed set.
    - A convertible pair converts through a declared factor, not an inferred one.
    - No arithmetic runs before validation.
  </acceptance_criteria>

  <name>Task 2: A total says what it left out</name>
  <files>custom_components/glt_flow_card/energy_model.py</files>
  <action>Fix D16, D17 and T7-12. An unavailable meter is silently skipped today, so a month with half the meters offline reports a smaller, confident cost; and CO₂ exists only for electricity, so gas and district heat vanish from a figure presented as the site's. Every total carries coverage and an explicit exclusion list with a reason per exclusion. The caller must be able to distinguish "no meters configured" from "no meters readable" — today both produce the same empty result.</action>
  <acceptance_criteria>
    - A total with an unavailable meter states its coverage and names the meter.
    - A CO₂ figure names the media it excludes.
    - "No meters configured" and "no meters readable" are different results.
  </acceptance_criteria>
  <verify><automated>py -3.13 -m pytest tests/components/glt_flow_card/test_energy_units.py -q -x</automated></verify>

  <name>Task 3: Circular quantities</name>
  <files>custom_components/glt_flow_card/energy_units.py</files>
  <action>Fix T7-13. The research found `StatisticMeanType.CIRCULAR` in the statistics metadata, alongside `ARITHMETIC` and `NONE`. An arithmetic mean of 350° and 10° is 180° — precisely the opposite of the truth, and a value nothing downstream would flag. Read the declared mean type and either average circularly or refuse; never average an angular quantity arithmetically. Read `mean_type` rather than the deprecated `has_mean`, which the research established is removed in 2026.4.</action>
  <acceptance_criteria>
