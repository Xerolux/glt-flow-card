# Phase 7 — Trends, Energy and Reports

**Status:** closed. 22 of 23 threats verified; T7-23 blocked by the environment,
recorded with its exact failure.

**Evidence at head:** 455 Node tests, 510 Python tests (1 deselected), 61
exact-dist browser tests, `verify-docs-site` 24 sources / 41 byte-identical site
files, HACS staging validated.

## What this phase was about

Phase 6's threats concerned effects that reach a human and fail silently.
Phase 7's concern **numbers that reach a human and are wrong in a plausible
direction**, which is harder to test for. A silent failure can be caught by
asserting that something happened; a plausible wrong number passes every
assertion of that kind. A straight line through a six-hour outage is not a
broken chart — it is a steady plant.

So the phase's subject is not trends. It is **what the product says about its
own answer**: coverage, units, period boundaries, bounds and provenance are the
outputs under test.

The single rule the surfaces enforce: **the screen never shows a number without
showing what it is a number of.** Value, unit, period and coverage travel
together or none of them appears.

## What shipped

**Honest history.** Four declared routes, enforced server-side, enumeration
*filtered* rather than denied (a refusal would itself tell an unauthorized
caller that rows exist), with the limit applied after filtering so it cannot
become a count oracle. Export is a **separate capability** from read: whoever
may look at the plant's history is not automatically whoever may carry it out of
the building.

**Local-calendar periods, resolved server-side.** A spring day is 23 hours, a
fall day 25, October 2027 is 745 hours. Proven against a corpus generated by
executing the vendored Home Assistant, so a divergence fails rather than drifts.
`day`/`week`/`month` go to `statistics_during_period`; `year` is reachable only
through `statistic_during_period`'s calendar spec — reading the plural command
alone concludes, wrongly, that the product must aggregate years itself.

**Two meter models, never converted into each other** — Phase 6's
interval-and-instant rule in a new subject. Meter resets stay the Recorder's
job: re-implementing reset detection would duplicate a well-tested
implementation and, since resets are rare and hard to fixture, get it wrong
unnoticed for months.

**Refuse, do not degrade** on units, carried from Phase 5. A wrong cost figure
is worse than a missing one.

**Reproducible reports**, with content-derived ids, and schedules validated at
authoring time and run by the *same* runner the plant schedules use — a second
scheduler would drift the first time one of them learned about DST.

**Six surfaces**, where a gap is a break rather than a line of any style,
coverage is stated even at 100 %, and every chart has a keyboard-reachable table
in which a gap is a marked row carrying its interval.

## The finding that mattered most

T7-22 was written to make "prove the retirement is *reached*" a blocking
requirement rather than a habit, because Phase 6 shipped exactly that defect one
layer out from where its own register drew the line.

**It caught Phase 7 doing the same thing.** The trend surfaces rendered a
confident zero because only the panel ever fetched anything, and `_seriesFor`
still called the retired `aggregateSeries` — so a plain render put an
epoch-aligned bucket on screen. An artifact grep passed throughout, because the
routes do appear in the bytes, in the one place nothing else reached.

Fixed in 07-19 and mutation-verified in both directions. `aggregateSeries` then
lost its last reference and the bundler dropped it: **the artifact can no longer
display a displaced bucket at all, rather than being trusted not to.**

## Limitations, including what this phase raised rather than took

- **`history/coverage` and `history/export` are shells** returning stated
  `unavailable`. 07-08 recorded four routes as shipped; three of them queried
  nothing. `history/statistics` was filled in 07-18 — its honesty
  (`source: "unavailable"`) was precisely why no string assertion could tell it
  from a working route, and the new test discriminates on the **gap**, which a
  route that never asked cannot produce. Coverage needs the period's expected
  instants, whose bucket step is a decision wanting a corpus behind it;
  inferring it from returned rows is the defect `expected_instants` exists to
  prevent, so it was **left open rather than half-built**.
- **Four or five alarm classes** still cannot be expressed (carried from Phase 6).
- **Measured capacity** at large plant sizes is Phase 10; the bounds constrain
  the *shape* of the cost, and the measured number is still outstanding.
- **Remote-site trends** are Phase 9.
- The exact-dist rows rest on the documented `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE`
  override, not on the bare owner command.
