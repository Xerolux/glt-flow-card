## The three things that decide the wave order

**Home Assistant already does most of this correctly, server-side.** The research
measured it: periods resolve on local-midnight boundaries with 23- and 25-hour
days and 743- and 745-hour months, and `change` is reset-aware. The product's job
is to *name, validate and render* a period, never to compute one, and not to
re-implement meter reset detection. Plans 07-06 and 07-12 depend on this.

**Coverage is the phase's central output.** Audit defects D1, D6, D7, D10, D16
and D18 are one defect in six costumes, each turning absent into a number, and
none producing a value an ordinary assertion would flinch at. 07-02 makes the
absent case expressible before anything else is built.

**Reachable is not reached.** T7-22 exists because Phase 6 shipped a retirement
whose artifact grep passed while three surfaces reported a confident zero. Every
retirement in this phase is paired with an outcome assertion, and 07-19 makes
that a blocking row rather than a habit.

## Environment limits carried in

- `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE` must be set: Playwright expects Chromium 1234
  and the container has 1194. Without it the browser suites fail as harness
  errors, and the RED gate correctly rejects them rather than counting them.
- `npm run test:phaseN` cannot complete: it recurses to Phase 1's `F-01`, whose
  `verify-provenance.mjs --online` leg needs `api.github.com`, which the egress
  proxy answers with 403 (`registry.npmjs.org` answers 200).
- `npm run test:phaseN:release` cannot run: `test:ha-artifacts` probes
  `docker info` for all twelve bounded lane candidates and this container has no
  Docker engine.

Both are environment, both are independent, and neither is evidence about the
code.
